# SNHU-CS255
CS-255 System Analysis and Design coursework featuring UML modeling and the DriverPass system design project.

## Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
The client for the DriverPass project was a company that wanted to provide students with online driver training and behind-the-wheel driving instruction. The system needed to allow customers to create accounts, purchase training packages, take practice tests, and schedule driving lessons. It also needed to support employees with different roles and access levels, maintain customer and appointment information, and receive updated testing information from the DMV.

## What did you do particularly well?
I think I did particularly well at taking the information provided by the client and turning it into specific system requirements. Rather than looking only at what the system needed to do, I also considered requirements such as security, role-based access, account protection, database storage, and the ability to adapt as the business changes. Creating the different UML diagrams also helped me look at the same system from several perspectives and make sure the individual parts worked together.

## If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
One area I would improve is the planning portion of my business requirements document. My Gantt chart showed the overall development schedule, but I could make it more useful by including more specific task durations and showing the dependencies between tasks more clearly. I would also consider adding more detail to the user interface requirements, particularly how the DriverPass system would communicate information and notifications to customers.

## How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?
I started by identifying what the different DriverPass users needed to accomplish and then translated those needs into functional and technical requirements. From there, I used the UML diagrams to show how customers, employees, administrators, and external systems would interact with the system. Considering the user's needs is important because a system can work perfectly from a technical standpoint and still fail if it is difficult to use or does not solve the problem the customer actually has.

## How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
I approach software design by first understanding the problem the system is supposed to solve before deciding how to build it. I would continue gathering requirements, separating functional and nonfunctional requirements, identifying the different users and their needs, and using diagrams to model the system before development begins. I would also continue reviewing the design throughout the process because requirements can change, and catching a problem during the design stage is much easier than fixing it after the system has already been built.
